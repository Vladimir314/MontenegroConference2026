Abstracts

Semyon Abramyan (ETH Zürich) — Embedding calculus for parallelized manifolds

We study a variant of the embedding functor \(Emb(M, N)\) that incorporates homotopical data from the frame bundle of the target manifold \(N\). Given a parallelized \(m\)-manifold \(M\) and an \(n\)-manifold \(N\) equipped with a section of its \(m\)-frame bundle, we define a modified embedding functor \(\widetilde{Emb}(M, N)\) that interpolates between the standard embedding and a reference framing. Using the manifold calculus of functors, we identify the Taylor tower of \(\widetilde{Emb}(M, N)\) with a mapping space of right modules over the Fulton–MacPherson operad. We prove a convergence theorem under a codimension condition, establishing a weak equivalence between \(\widetilde{Emb}(M, N)\) and its Taylor approximation. Finally, under rationalization, we describe the derived mapping space in terms of a combinatorial hairy graph complex, enabling computational access to the rational homotopy type of the space of embeddings.

Anshu Agarwal (Indian Institute of Technology Delhi) — Regular genus of small covers over \(\Delta^{n-1} \times I\) and \(\Delta^2 \times \Delta^2\)

A crystallization of a PL manifold is an edge-colored graph encoding a contracted triangulation of the manifold. The concept of regular genus generalizes the notions of surface genus and Heegaard genus for 3-manifolds to higher-dimensional closed PL manifolds. The regular genus of a PL manifold is a PL invariant. Determining the regular genus of a closed PL \(n\)-manifold remains a fundamental challenge in combinatorial topology. Small covers are closed \(n\)-manifolds admitting a locally standard \(\mathbb{Z}_2^n\)-action with orbit space homeomorphic to a simple convex polytope \(P^n\). In this talk, we construct the crystallizations of small covers over \(\Delta^{n-1} \times I\) and \(\Delta^2 \times \Delta^2\). We demonstrate that there are exactly \(1 + 2^{n-1}\) D-J equivalence classes of small covers over the prism \(\Delta^{n-1} \times I\), where \(n\geq 3\). For each \(\mathbb{Z}_2\)-characteristic function of \(\Delta^{n-1} \times I\), we construct a \(2^{n-1}(n+1)\)-vertex crystallization of the small cover \(M^n(\lambda)\) with regular genus \(1 + 2^{n-4}(n^2 - 2n - 3)\), where \(n\geq 4\). For the polytope \(P = \Delta^2 \times \Delta^2\), we classify all the small covers up to Davis-Januszkiewicz (D-J) equivalence and show that there are exactly seven such covers. Among these, one is \(\mathbb{RP}^2 \times \mathbb{RP}^2\), while the others are \(\mathbb{RP}^2\)-bundles over \(\mathbb{RP}^2\). Remarkably, each of these seven small covers has the regular genus 8.

Stoyan Apostolov (Sofia University, Sofia, Bulgaria) — Axiomatic degree theory for topological manifolds

We study axiomatization of degree theory for topological manifolds, similar to the one on Euclidean spaces, as elaborated in the book of Deimling, Nonlinear functional analysis. Our construction however differs, relying not on smooth approximations as Deimling, but cohomological methods. We study a generalization of the winding number concept. We also study a relevant product formula.

Đorđe Baralić (Mathematical Institute SANU) — Universal simplicial complexes and the Buchstaber invariant

In this lecture, we present recent results on universal simplicial complexes, their generalisation to the field \(\mathbf{F}_p\) for a prime \(p\), and the associated Buchstaber invariants. The talk is based on joint work with Aleš Vavpetič and Aleksandar Vučić.

Ulrich Bauer (Technical University of Munich) — Persistence beyond data analysis: new interactions with geometry, topology, and algebra

Persistent homology is usually presented as a tool for extracting stable topological information from noisy data. In this talk, I will focus on how ideas and questions arising in topological data analysis lead to new results and perspectives in pure mathematics.

After recalling the basic structure and stability theory of one-parameter persistence, I will discuss three examples. The first concerns Vietoris–Rips complexes and Gromov hyperbolicity, where the perspective of persistent homology leads to refinements of classical results about the topology of Rips complexes and connects them to discrete Morse theory and computation. The second concerns Morse's functional topology, where persistence provides a rigorous framework for generalized Morse inequalities and for the classical argument of Morse and Tompkins concerning unstable minimal surfaces. Finally, I will indicate how persistence modules, viewed as representations of posets equipped with a metric notion of perturbation, lead to new questions about decomposition and stability in representation theory.

These examples illustrate how persistent homology, beyond its role in the analysis of data, provides new viewpoints and methods in geometry, topology, and algebra.

Madhusudan Deval (Banaras Hindu University) — Some aspects of the TD axiom, sobriety and weak sobriety for topological systems

This paper systematically examines the analogue of the TD separation axiom for topological systems by comparing it with existing separation axioms. We discuss its properties and consequences for certain types of completely prime filters, as well as the internal structure of the frame associated with the topological system. Furthermore, we explore sobriety and weak sobriety of a topological system using the true-kernel and the false-kernel of a point of the frame associated to that topological system.

Vladimir Dragović (The University of Texas at Dallas) — Complexity of Lasso, Geometry and Combinatorics

We observe and prove that the complexity of lasso for normalized data is smaller than for nonnormalized ones by relating this question to extremal combinatorics and algebraic graph theory. We employ a geometric approach to the lasso as a study of the tangency of the level sets of the least square objective function with the polyhedral boundary sets \(B(t)\) of the parameters in \(\mathbb R^p\) with the \(\ell_1\) norm equal to \(t\). We geometrically derive closed exact formulae for the solution of the lasso under the full rank assumption. We establish important general properties of the solutions of the lasso, which are known to be represented as a simple polygonal chain in \(\mathbb{R}^p\). Starting from \(p=2\) and \(p=3\), we show a striking difference in the maximal number of \(p\)-dimensional orthants a polygonal chain of a lasso solution can intersect in the case of normalized data vs. nonnormalized data. We prove that in the normalized case, the number \(h_{p,2}\) is a general upper bound for the number of segments of a lasso solution intersecting a \(p\)-dimensional orthant, where \(h_{p,r}\) is the maximal number of binary words of length \(p\) such that every two words match at least at \(r\) spots, \(r\le p\). We calculate \(h_{p,2}\) using spectral graph theory. It was known that for general data the sharp estimate for that number is \(2^{p-1}\), which we identify with \(h_{p,1}\). We also find an upper bound for the total number of segments of a lasso solution with normalized data in dimension \(p\), that is significantly less than \((3^p+1)/2\), a well-known sharp upper bound for the nonnormalized case. This is based on the joint work with Borislav Gajić.

Nilay Ekiz Yazıcı (Bursa Technical University) — \(m\)-Contiguity Distance

In this study, the \(m\)-contiguity distance is introduced as an approximation of the contiguity distance between simplicial maps, and its fundamental properties are investigated. In addition, its relationships with the simplicial Lusternik--Schnirelmann category and discrete topological complexity are studied, together with its behavior under barycentric subdivision, strong homotopy equivalence, and in the context of aspherical spaces.

Ioannis Gkeneralis (Aristotle University of Thessaloniki) — How much of the homeomorphism type is determined in toric topology?

Toric topology studies spaces whose topology is encoded by combinatorial data such as simplicial complexes and simple polytopes. A fundamental question is to what extent this combinatorial information determines the topology of the resulting spaces. More generally, one may ask which topological invariants are sufficient to recover the homeomorphism type of a manifold arising in toric topology.

In this talk, we discuss several rigidity phenomena from this perspective. We begin with quasitoric and quoric manifolds, whose orbit-space descriptions lead to strong classification results. We then turn to moment--angle manifolds and explain how their topology can often be understood through associated principal bundles over quasitoric or quoric quotients. In the complex and quaternionic settings, this viewpoint leads to equivariant rigidity results, reducing classification problems to the study of characteristic classes of the corresponding kernel bundles.

We also discuss the real case, where flag simplicial complexes give rise to CAT(0) cubical geometry through the Davis complex. This provides a connection between toric topology and geometric rigidity phenomena related to the Farrell--Jones and Borel conjectures.

The aim of the talk is to illustrate how combinatorics, geometry, and topology interact in determining the homeomorphism type of spaces arising in toric topology.

Jelena Grbić (University of Southampton, UK) — Topology and Combinatorics Uncovering Structures of Vietoris-Rips Complexes

I will explore the homotopy theory of Vietris-Rips complexes of hypercubes, focusing on the deep interplay between their topology and combinatorial structures. The goal is to understand key structural properties, such as higher connectivity, co-connectivity and the realisation of homotopy types, by introducing a new combinatorial-topological framework.

This is party joint work with Martin Bendersky and Salvatore Elia.

Doron Grossman-Naples (University of Illinois, Urbana-Champaign) — Isogenies and Hecke Operators for Elliptic Cohomology

Operations on tempered cohomology theories (and complex-periodic cohomology theories more generally) are known to be intimately related to the isogenies of their associated algebraic groups. The Adams operations on K-theory, for example, are associated with the \(k\)th power map on \(\mathbb{G}_m\). Their analogues at height 2, Hecke operators on elliptic cohomology, are not as well-understood.

In this talk, I will describe a spectral algebreo-geometric approach to studying these operators. This begins with the spectral moduli stack of isogenies of oriented elliptic curves, which I show to be a (nonconnective) spectral Deligne-Mumford stack using a connected-étale decomposition and a generalization of Lurie's theorem on the representability of derived Hilbert spaces. We will then apply this to construct stacky Hecke operators in a manner similar to their classical moduli-theoretic construction—with the notable caveat that in the spectral case, Grothendieck duality introduces a nontrivial twist.

Sudarshan Gurjar (IIT Bombay) — Eilenberg–MacLane and Moore spaces in algebraic surface theory

The proposed talk will focus on the topology of non-singular complex algebraic surfaces, both affine and projective. Closely related is the topology of the universal of these spaces. In many situations, this universal cover turns out to be either an Eilenberg-MacLane space or a Moore space. I will discuss these properties and some related classification theorems. Special focus will be on surfaces fibered over a curve. For such surfaces one has a good information about its topology (and that of its covering) in terms of the knowledge of the base, the general fiber and the nature of the singular fibers.

Vladimir Ivanović (University of Montenegro) — Moduli space of weighted pointed stable curves and toric topology of Grassmann manifolds \(G_{n,2}\) for \(n=5,6\)

There is defined the canonical action of the compact torus \(T^n\) on the complex Grassmann manifold \(G_{n,2}\). Our starting point is the \((U_n,p_n)\) model for \(X_n\) constructed by Buchstaber and Terzić (2022), where \(U_n = \Delta_{n,2}\times\mathcal F_n\) for the hypersimplex \(\Delta_{n,2}\) and the universal parameter space \(\mathcal F_n\) as defined in the works of Buchstaber and Terzić (2019, 2022). Buchstaber and Terzić (2023) proved that \(\mathcal F_n\) is diffeomorphic to the moduli space \(\mathcal M_{0,n}\) of stable rational curves of genus zero with \(n\) fixed ordered points. For the cases \(n = 5\) and \(n = 6\), we provide a description of the model \(U_n\) in terms of the hyperplane arrangement \(\mathcal{G}(n,2)\). We apply the resulting description to classify the Hassett spaces \(\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathcal{A}}\) for the case \(n = 5\). Based on joint work with Svjetlana Terzic.

Sumandeep Kaur (Shanghai University) — Arithmetic Invariants of Number Fields

Arithmetic invariants such as discriminants, index, and integral bases provide fundamental information about the arithmetic structure of algebraic number fields. In this talk, we investigate the explicit determination of these invariants through a local-to-global approach. For each relevant prime, we study the p-adic behavior of the discriminant and construct corresponding p-integral bases, which provide detailed information about the local structure of the ring of integers. The talk highlights the role of local arithmetic methods in determining global invariants and illustrates how p-adic and geometric constructions such as Newton polygons can reveal the underlying structure of algebraic number fields.

Shintaro Kuroki (Okayama University of Science) — Locally standard T-pseudomanifolds and their cohomology

In our previous work (arXiv:2511.10153), we introduced the notion of a locally standard T-pseudomanifold and obtained a classification under suitable assumptions. This class includes both quasitoric manifolds and projective toric varieties. In this talk, we introduce explicit descriptions of the equivariant intersection cohomology of certain locally standard T-pseudomanifolds. This is joint work with Yuya Koike.

Mikiya Masuda (Osaka Metropolitan University) — Symmetric matrices defined by plane vector sequences

Motivated by a work of Fu-So-Song, we associate a symmetric matrix A to a plane vector sequence v and give a formula to find the signature of A in terms of the sequence v. When A is nonsingular, we interpret the relation between A and A^{-1} from a topological viewpoint. In fact, one can associate an omnioriented quasitoric orbifold X of real dimension four to the sequence v and see that A^{-1} is the intersection matrix of the characteristic suborbifolds of X.

Petar Pavešić (University of Ljubljana and Institute of Mathematics, Physics and Mechanics, Ljubljana) — Parametrized LS category and group actions

We introduce a new invariant associated to an action of a group \(G\) on a (possibly non-commutative) group \(K\). The invariant is based on the parametrized LS-category of the semi-direct product \(K\rtimes G\) over \(G\) and it also reflects a choice of a section, given by a derivation \(d\colon G\to K\). The invariant is denoted \(\mathrm{tc}(d)\) and takes integer values between \(\mathrm{cd}(K)\) and \(\mathrm{cd}(K)+\mathrm{cd}(G)\), reflecting the intricacies of the action of \(G\) on \(K\). We compute its values for semi-direct product decompositions of fundamental groups of mapping tori, of pure braid groups and of iterated semi-direct products of free groups. To achieve this, we relate \(\mathrm{tc}(d)\) to the cohomological dimension of its factors, to the sectional category of the inclusion of \(G\) in \(K\rtimes G\), and to the properties of torsors that are associated to the derivation \(d\).

This is a joint work with U. Ogrinec.

Stephen Theriault (University of Southampton) — Moment-angle manifolds associated to neighbourly triangulations of spheres

Work of McGavran, Bosio-Meersseman and Gitler-Lopez de Medrano shows that a moment-angle manifold associated to a neighbourly triangulation of an odd dimensional sphere that is polytopal is diffeomorphic to a connected sum of products of two spheres. It has been an open problem for several years to give a homotopy theoretic proof that allows for an extension to non-polytopal cases. In this talk a solution to the problem is discussed. This is joint work with Amaranta Membrillo-Solis.

Rade Živaljević (Mathematical Institute SANU, Belgrade) — Combinatorics, geometry, and topology of Bier spheres

Bier spheres, named after Thomas Bier, have been studied from the viewpoint of combinatorics (simplicial complexes), topology (polyhedral products, toric manifolds), convex polytopes (generalized permutohedra, algorithmic Steinitz problem), game theory (cooperative games), experimental mathematics (nonpolytopal spheres), combinatorial optimization (submodular functions), algebraic statistics (convex rank tests), etc.

We present an overview of selected results from the project ``Bier-2016-2026'', as initiated at ICERM (2016), emphasizing the interplay of ideas from different mathematical fields. One of the cornerstone results is the following theorem, which links Bier spheres with braid arrangements, polymatroids, and generalized permutahedra. This result, together with its consequences and ramifications, paves the way for many applications, from polyhedral combinatorics to toric geometry and topology.

Theorem 1. Each Bier sphere admits a canonical star-shaped geometric realization whose radial fan coarsens the braid arrangement fan. Moreover, this fan is unimodular in the lattice \(A_n^\ast\), dual to the root lattice \(A_n\).

The talk is based on the past, present and future collaboration with participants and friends of Belgrade ``CGTA-project'' including: Siniša Vrećica, Duško Jojić, Gaiane Panina, Vladimir Grujić, Đorđe Baralić, Marinko Timotijević, Filip Jevtić, Ivan Limonchenko, Marija Jelić Milutinović, and many others.
